# Field findings — live fire, 2026-09-05

Measured, not inferred, unless marked. Kept because several of these cost a day to learn and
every one of them is a trap that looks like a result.

## Detection

The upstream detector was a **level gate** — amplitude over ambient, no gunshot logic at all.
It emitted **427 events for ~60 rounds**. Breakdown over 1014 board-events:

- **60%** fired within 60 ms of the previous one — inside a single blast's own decay
- **16%** at 60–400 ms
- **14%** had rise times over 5 ms, up to 313 ms — far too slow for a muzzle blast

A guard interval shorter than the delay of any reflector past ~4 m counts reflections as new
events *by construction*.

## Classifier

Six features (rise, decay, centroid, crest, >2 kHz fraction, peak), logistic, **grouped-CV
AUC 0.959** on 228 operator labels against a 0.561 majority baseline. Applied to all events and
merged across boards at 60 ms: **70 rounds in 48 strings** vs the operator's own count of ~60.

⚠️**Ablate; do not trust permutation importance.** It reported `peak` as 30× everything else.
Ablation: peak alone 0.873, shape features *without* peak 0.879, together 0.959. It had handed
shared signal to one correlated feature.

⚠️**`gap` was dropped.** 80.5% of events have gap under 1 s contributing <0.001 logits, while
the 6.4% over 10 s reach +2.55 at p99 — that tail encodes when the operator paused between
strings. It also made every capture's first event unscoreable (no predecessor → `gap=None`),
silently refusing the most important round in a string. Cost of removal: AUC 0.9601 → 0.9589,
accuracy unchanged.

## The array hears the CRACK, not the blast

Operator labels: 92 crack, 8 crack+thump, **0 blast-only**, 112 noise. The spectrum agrees
independently — centroid ~7.9 kHz, 1% of energy below 500 Hz, 47% above 2 kHz. The rounds were
suppressed 5.56 M855; a can silences the blast and cannot touch the shockwave.

## Geometry

- M855 at ~900 m/s, 23 °C (c = 345.24 m/s): M = 2.61, cone half-angle **22.6°**, so the crack
  arrives **67.4° off the trajectory**. Bearing-to-shooter from a crack is wrong by that angle.
- ⚠️**Offset is unobservable from same-side nodes.** Shifting a track ±6 m past three same-side
  nodes changed the TDoAs by **0.000 ms**; past straddling nodes, by up to **117 ms**. Every
  parallel track fits identically, at zero residual. **Sensor placement, not sensor quality, is
  the binding constraint.**
- ⚠️**Three nodes exactly determine a 2D trajectory** (2 equations, 2 unknowns), so the residual
  is ~0 by construction and proves nothing. Four nodes is where a residual starts meaning
  something.

## Instrument limits

- **N-wave is 3–9 samples at 48 kHz** (57.6 µs at 1 m miss, 182 µs at 100 m). Analytic N-wave
  detection failed for this reason, not for want of a better algorithm. 192 kHz is the practical
  floor for shape.
- MEMS mics are **AC-coupled**: they differentiate a pressure step rather than reproduce it.
- **15% of events clipped.** Standard MEMS AOP is 120–130 dB SPL; a rifle report is ~160 dB at
  1 m and 125–135 dB at 50 m. This is an overload-point limit, not a gain setting.
- Echo tail is **diffuse, not specular** — max 5.5σ over a smooth trend, so no discrete
  reflectors are resolvable. Real reverb to ~14 m of excess path, then the site's noise floor.
  ⚠️An RT60 of 0.85 s computed here was **invalid** — Schroeder integration ran into that noise
  floor without Lundeby truncation.

## Timebase

Cross-board offsets of 25–88 ms traced to `--max-lag-ms 400` **in a systemd unit, not the
script**, trimming whole 80 ms blocks against a ~328 ms burst cadence. The script on disk was
byte-identical to the repo, so a diff proved nothing. Fixed → 0 drops, and right↔left improved
30×. A residual per-board startup offset remains.

⚠️Recording only a ring's single anchor makes a capture **uninterpretable per board afterwards**.
The offsets were knowable at capture time and simply were not written down.

## The array validity gate (`hear/solve/consistency.py`)

Three arrays produced bearings that were fiction and nothing caught it. What catches it costs
nothing and needs no ground truth: pairwise delays are generated by N arrival times, so they live
in N−1 dimensions, not C(N,2). Every loop must close. **Measured over 205 unclipped Kinect
impulses**, 30 µs tolerance (half a sample at 16 kHz), each pair's band already clamped to its
own spatial Nyquist:

| check | pass | rate |
|---|---|---|
| worst loop over the field basis {012,123,013} | 4 / 205 | 2.0% |
| worst loop over the module's basis {012,013,023} | 5 / 205 | 2.4% |
| worst loop over all four triangles | 0 / 205 | 0.0% |
| worst **pair** off the best-fit arrival times (`closure_residual`) | 31 / 205 | 15.1% |
| the full verdict — closure **and** the |τ| ≤ d/c bound | 17 / 205 | 8.3% |
| the full verdict with the band **not** clamped per pair | 0 / 205 | 0.0% |

⚠️**The published 2% is basis-dependent.** A loop residual sums up to three pair errors, so the
same delays score 2–3× worse as a loop than as a pair, and which three loops you happen to pick
moves the count. A tolerance of half a correlator sample is a statement about one pair, so the
gate is on the pair: `closure_residual` asks whether *any* set of arrival times explains the
measured delays, which has no basis to choose.

⚠️**The physical bound is the most productive single check.** 117 of 205 impulses (57%) report a
delay no direction of arrival can produce, |τ| > d/c. It only bites because the correlator was
allowed to search ±1.15·d/c: a search window clamped to the bound cannot report an impossible
delay, it reports a wrong possible one instead.

⚠️**What additivity cannot see.** It never mentions the geometry, so a mismeasured baseline is
perfectly consistent and perfectly wrong — relevant here, since `KINECT_MIC_Y` is marked
"APPROXIMATE, NEVER MEASURED". And a per-channel constant timing offset cancels in every loop, so
channel skew is invisible to it. Both are only reachable with a trusted survey, via the opt-in
affine (plane-wave) check.

⚠️**Gated events are not an unbiased sample of directions.** 19% of the 205 impulses sit near
endfire (|direction cosine| > 0.7); **none** of the 17 survivors do — every one lies 58–117° off
the array axis. Near endfire the true delay is already at the edge of the lag window, so a
peak-pick error crosses the bound instead of merely widening the residual. A bearing histogram
built from gated events will lean broadside.
